The right layer.
In the right room.

Blackout where people sleep or watch movies; sheers with a separate privacy layer in social rooms; compact practical shades in working and wet areas. Sheers alone are reserved for glazing verified to have no privacy or blackout requirement.

One palette; different functions.

Warm ivory linen-look sheers, ivory or oatmeal blackout curtain faces, and quiet warm-neutral shades. Concealed or slim dark tracks coordinate with walnut, natural oak and restrained black details. Blackout describes the backing, not a black visible curtain.

MW-A02 corrects visible openings against original photographs and the blueprint. Day shows open layers; night demonstrates privacy/blackout closed where visible. Bedroom 2 follows the clarified stair-hall/closet layout; Bedroom 5 reverse view interprets its planned front windows. Rendered operation is not a tested privacy or blackout result.

Sheers soften daylight; privacy is separate

Keep the airy sheer appearance in living rooms, but include an independently operated, non-see-through privacy shade behind it. An open-weave solar screen is not a substitute for an evening privacy layer. Test the actual sample from outside with the room lights on; silhouettes can remain visible through some light-filtering fabrics.

Blackout requires coverage as well as fabric

For dual curtains, place sheers closest to the glass and fully closing blackout-lined panels on an independent room-side track. Resolve center overlap, wall returns and top/bottom light gaps. Where furniture prevents functional curtains, substitute a blackout roller behind the sheers. Fabric alone does not guarantee a completely dark room.

When just sheers are enough

Only where an after-dark sightline check confirms no privacy need and the room has no sleep or cinema requirement. No occupied bedroom or street-facing social room is assigned sheers alone by default. Verified private high glazing can remain bare; a sheer-only treatment there is an aesthetic choice.

Keep the construction simple

Base pricing assumes manual tracks and cordless shades, retail or semi-custom fabric, and surface-mounted hardware. No rebuilt ceiling pockets or motors. Measure track width, stack-back, opening operation, furniture and floor variation; choose fullness with the fabricator for the actual heading and fabric. Avoid puddled fabric.

04 / Keep the character

Spend on what you feel.

Keep the walnut, mineral, ivory and natural-oak relationship. Simplify quantities and fabrication before compromising installation quality.

Protect the design, simplify the fabrication

Use stock cabinet boxes with good walnut veneer fronts and clean edge details. Quote an office wall and bar in modules before choosing full bespoke cabinetry. This is already the base strategy; do not subtract a second saving.

Keep specialty paint selective

The base covers 600 sf of focal-wall treatment. Reducing to 300 sf saves about $4,140–7,450 including modeled GC and contingency; use washable matte paint on the balance.

Buy comfort before a label

The base furniture allowance uses quality retail alternatives. Exact RH Cloud or other premium selections need fresh SKU prices. Spend on sofa comfort, mattresses and durable upholstery first.

Phase optional purchases

Delay the baby grand, terrace set or guest-room upgrades if needed. This lowers the initial cash requirement, not the final cost if they are bought later.

Replacement scope drives the total

Keeping serviceable kitchen/bath cabinetry or intact bathroom tile could lower the project materially, but that would change the requested replacement assumption. Obtain retain/reface/replace alternates from the same GC instead of applying an arbitrary discount.

05 / Homeowner + general contractor

Make every bid comparable.

You do not need to hire an interior designer for this plan. You do need measured information, coordinated products and a clear purchasing split.

  • Issue the same room scope, sample board and allowance schedule to three GCs. Ask for exclusions, subcontractor scope, management markup and change-order rates.
  • You select and buy scheduled furniture, decorative lights, drapery, appliances and AV. The GC buys construction materials, cabinetry, tile and plumbing fixtures unless a written bid transfers a specific item to you.
  • For owner-supplied fixtures/appliances, agree who measures, approves cut sheets, receives/checks damage, stores, installs and handles warranty/service before purchase. Remove the exact allowance from the GC bid if procurement responsibility changes.
  • Approve lighting/AV circuits and cabinet shop drawings before walls close. Order long-lead fixtures to the contractor schedule; furniture can follow dusty work.
  • Track contract totals, actual owner orders and remaining allowances separately. Hold contingency outside the base contract and approve change orders in writing.

Give the GC these together

This allowance schedule, the original Option 1 room views, room-by-room material notes, a measured plan, selected finish samples and a list of open decisions. Renders communicate intent; cabinet shop drawings and product cut sheets establish fabrication sizes.
