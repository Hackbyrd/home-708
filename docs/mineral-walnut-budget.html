Quantity and scope assumptions
  • 3,000 net sf of new dry-area flooring and 600 sf of specialty wall finish are adjustable assumptions. The reported gross house area is not a flooring takeoff.
  • About 26 window/door treatment groups, roughly 40 dry-area lighting/control points and 100 ft of LED are scope placeholders. Get measurements and a circuit schedule.
  • Kitchen, four bathroom service layouts, windows, stairs and structural walls remain in place. Bath 4 is assumed to have an existing enclosure/services; its condition is unverified.
  • Bedroom 5 is confirmed unbuilt. Base includes a modest interior enclosure, not an addition, roof alteration or extensive HVAC upgrade.
  • Good-quality retail upholstery and veneered walnut joinery, selective semi-custom cabinetry, manual drapes and porcelain/quartz substitutes are assumed; exact luxury-brand replication is not priced.
  • New wood floors in all four bedrooms with loose rugs. Gym remains empty except floor, mirror, walls and quote.
What the arithmetic means
  • All line items are our estimates; linked market examples are benchmarks with different scopes, not bids for this house.
  • Construction rows are direct trade labor/material allowances with contractor-purchased material tax included. Add the modeled GC markup once to Construction only; do not add it again to Planning or owner purchases.
  • Owner purchases are goods before tax/delivery. Add modeled 6% Maryland sales/use tax to those goods only. Add 5% purchase logistics for delivery, assembly, returns and specialist piano handling as an allowance, not a vendor fee schedule.
  • 15% contingency applies to the subtotal including GC, owner goods, tax and logistics. It is a reserve, not promised spending or a guarantee against all unknowns.
  • Furniture rows exclude decorative lights, drapery, art/plants, AV and appliances, which are separate shared rows. Baths include plumbing fixtures and mirrors but exclude decorative light-fixture purchase. Flooring and ordinary dry-area paint are counted once in shared rows.
  • No designer/design-build fee, owner-furniture credit, loan interest or financing cost included. Homeowner coordination time is unpaid and not priced.
  • A lower figure means simpler products, competitive trade quotes and uncomplicated conditions; the upper figure allows more expensive selections/preparation within this scope. This is not a statistical confidence interval.
Not included in the base
  • Exterior/roof/window replacement, deck waterproofing, structural alterations, extensive service relocations, electrical panel/HVAC plant replacement and major concealed damage.
  • Garage works, elevator, unrendered powder/pantry/utility-room remodels beyond shared dry-area paint, moving/storage or temporary accommodation.
  • New Bath 4 enclosure/services if absent; bathroom heated floors, whole-house audio, motorized drapes, gym equipment and premium custom millwork throughout.
  • Professional art originals, new fireplace appliances, actual RH/other premium model upgrades beyond the allowances, and sales financing.

04 / Keep the character

Spend on what you feel.

Keep the walnut, mineral, ivory and natural-oak relationship. Simplify quantities and fabrication before compromising installation quality.

Protect the design, simplify the fabrication

Use stock cabinet boxes with good walnut veneer fronts and clean edge details. Quote an office wall and bar in modules before choosing full bespoke cabinetry. This is already the base strategy; do not subtract a second saving.

Keep specialty paint selective

The base covers 600 sf of focal-wall treatment. Reducing to 300 sf saves about $4,140–7,450 including modeled GC and contingency; use washable matte paint on the balance.

Buy comfort before a label

The base furniture allowance uses quality retail alternatives. Exact RH Cloud or other premium selections need fresh SKU prices. Spend on sofa comfort, mattresses and durable upholstery first.

Phase optional purchases

Delay the baby grand, terrace set or guest-room upgrades if needed. This lowers the initial cash requirement, not the final cost if they are bought later.

Replacement scope drives the total

Keeping serviceable kitchen/bath cabinetry or intact bathroom tile could lower the project materially, but that would change the requested replacement assumption. Obtain retain/reface/replace alternates from the same GC instead of applying an arbitrary discount.

Price these separately

Heated floors in two third-floor baths

$4,000–8,000 installed before project contingency

Separate alternate. Local contractor benchmark is $1,800–4,000 per room; verify heated area, circuit and thermostat requirements.

Whole-house audio including terrace

$6,000–15,000 additional planning allowance

Avoid duplicating media-room components already in base; topology, weather rating and zone count unselected.

05 / Homeowner + general contractor

Make every bid comparable.

You do not need to hire an interior designer for this plan. You do need measured information, coordinated products and a clear purchasing split.

  • Issue the same room scope, sample board and allowance schedule to three GCs. Ask for exclusions, subcontractor scope, management markup and change-order rates.
  • You select and buy scheduled furniture, decorative lights, drapery, appliances and AV. The GC buys construction materials, cabinetry, tile and plumbing fixtures unless a written bid transfers a specific item to you.
  • For owner-supplied fixtures/appliances, agree who measures, approves cut sheets, receives/checks damage, stores, installs and handles warranty/service before purchase. Remove the exact allowance from the GC bid if procurement responsibility changes.
  • Approve lighting/AV circuits and cabinet shop drawings before walls close. Order long-lead fixtures to the contractor schedule; furniture can follow dusty work.
  • Track contract totals, actual owner orders and remaining allowances separately. Hold contingency outside the base contract and approve change orders in writing.

Give the GC these together

This allowance schedule, the original Option 1 room views, room-by-room material notes, a measured plan, selected finish samples and a list of open decisions. Renders communicate intent; cabinet shop drawings and product cut sheets establish fabrication sizes.
