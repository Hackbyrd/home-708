05 / The correct order

Design furniture now.
Deliver most of it later.

New framing comes before rough wiring; wood flooring comes after wet and dusty work. Ordinary wall paint is relatively changeable. Flooring, concealed services and fitted cabinetry deserve the earliest decisions.

  1. Design + measure

    Set room uses, furniture dimensions, cabinetry, lighting and curtain stack positions before rough-in. Buying furniture later does not mean designing it later.

  2. Approvals + selective demolition

    Obtain the applicable permits; remove only the scope that can be completed. Solve leaks and substrate problems before decorative work.

  3. Framing + backing

    Frame approved wall changes first. Prepare mounting/blocking and track supports while open; preserve the photographed architecture.

  4. Rough services + inspections

    Electrical/low voltage, plumbing, HVAC and any required fire-system coordination. Inspect, test, label and photograph before concealment.

  5. Close walls + wet work

    Insulation and drywall after inspection; waterproofing/tile and substrate preparation with the required tests and drying time.

  6. Essential cabinets + main paint

    Fixed cabinet placement and most coatings before wood where the selected system requires it. Template counters after cabinets. Specific product instructions govern exceptions.

  7. Wood + stairs + trim

    Dry conditioned site, documented substrate tests, installation, trim and touchups. Protect new surfaces.

  8. Finish services + coverings

    Lights, controls, appliances, final connections and inspections. Then clean and fit functional window layers. Protect the finished exposed-wood stairs through move-in.

  9. Furniture + styling

    Move-in essentials first; final sofas, rugs, art, lamps and accessories can follow from the frozen palette and dimension schedule.

Cabinet/floor sequencing varies by installation system. Floating floors must retain movement freedom; confirm the actual floor manufacturer's requirements before installing fixed cabinetry. Manufacturer example ↗

06 / Freeze the enduring decisions

One palette.
Room to collect.

One physical finish board

Natural matte oak, warm ivory/mushroom, coordinated walnut, quiet black accents, ivory/oatmeal textiles and plants. Test large paint/mineral samples next to actual wood and fabric by day and under selected lamps. Renders are not color specifications.

Floor product and junctions

Lock brand/SKU, width, sheen, grade, installation method, transition details and stair relationship. Confirm any acoustic/HOA requirements. Survey quantities; reserve matching stock for repairs and the deferred penthouse only after its plan/quantity is fixed and suitable storage is available.

Furniture footprints before outlets

Record bed/mattress and headboard width, floating bedside positions, TV heights, 83-inch media TV, sofa envelope, office desk orientation, dining-table/pendant center and piano access. Exact upholstery can wait; anything that fixes electrical or cabinet geometry cannot.

Light quality and controls

Test actual fixtures and dimmers; define arrival, task, conversation, movie, reading and cleaning scenes. Coordinate driver access, recesses and high-access chandelier support before closure. Do not buy all decorative fittings without checking actual lead times and mounting needs.

Window layers and future curtains

Decide track type, number of layers, return/stack space and any future motor-power position before drywall. Final fabric lengths are measured from finished tracks and floors. Full decorative drapes may wait; sleeping darkness and nighttime privacy cannot.

Joinery and future matching

Keep a labeled approved walnut control sample, finish recipe, cabinet shop drawings, hardware schedule and photos. Later cabinets can vary in form while matching the same finish family; do not rely on a stain name or a screen image.

What can remain flexible

Artwork, plants, cushions, occasional tables, lamps and most rugs. Ordinary wall paint is relatively easy to change; flooring, concealed services, stone, cabinet geometry and built-in finish details deserve the earliest decisions.

08 / Give this to your GC

One base scope. Two separately priced options.

Quote Option 1 as three separate scopes: A—essential April 1 occupancy; B—optional work that may proceed only if price, approvals and timing fit; C—post-move phases. Provide a trade sequence, long-lead register, required inspection list, owner-purchase deadlines, protected access plan and payment milestones. Complete disruptive work in occupied areas before handover. Do not demolish a deferred room or cover uninspected work. A change must state both its dollar impact and effect on the move-in date before approval.

Ask for a written, licensed-contractor agreement with clear start/completion targets and progress milestones. Maryland limits the initial home-improvement contractor deposit to one-third and requires the contract to be signed before payment. That is a maximum, not a recommended deposit.
