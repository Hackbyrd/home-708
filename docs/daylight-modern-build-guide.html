Daylight Modern

A modern showcase home with hotel comfort, built in sensible phases.

Use the abundant daylight as the main feature. Warm white, mushroom cabinetry, natural oak and quiet stone textures create a consistent background. Comfortable upholstery, generous curtains and carefully placed warm light supply the hotel feeling. The expensive-looking details are alignment, scale and finish quality.

Daylight and evening comfort

Control glare without losing the windows

Fit inside-mount light-filtering or solar rollers where glare matters, with curtains stacking beside the glass. Use room-darkening/blackout layers in bedrooms and media. Sheers and solar fabrics alone do not provide dependable night privacy.

Keep surfaces quiet

Choose washable matte walls, matte/satin wood and calm stone patterns. Avoid glossy floors and large polished surfaces opposite intense sunlight. Read finish samples on more than one wall before buying.

Zone by exposure, not one setting

Survey which windows receive morning versus afternoon sun. Start with manual shades; separately control the sunroom, main living room and owner bedroom. Keep daytime shades partly open as conditions allow.

A separate evening scene

Use 2700K dimmable decorative lighting in living/bedrooms and consistent 3000K task light in kitchen/baths, with CRI 90+ as the selection target. Aim for light on faces, art and work surfaces; use the existing ceiling locations where possible.

Protect materials thoughtfully

Choose manufacturer-approved finishes and upholstery for the actual exposure; move/rotate rugs when practical. Consider window film only after the glazing manufacturer confirms compatibility. Do not promise fade-proof wood or fabrics.

W1 · Dry-area floors, island panels, selected storage

Natural oak

Neutral natural-oak engineered planks, roughly 5–7 inches wide, matte factory finish, restrained knots; select substrate, wear layer and installation system with the flooring contractor. Oak-veneer panels on stable cores for visible cabinetry.

Budget choice: Straight-laid stock planks and veneer panels; skip herringbone and solid-oak cabinets.

S1 · Existing fireplace surround, optional bath feature walls and dry floors

Quiet stone effect

Warm ivory/limestone-look matte porcelain, typically 12×24 or 24×48 where substrate flatness and installer capability permit; coordinated grout and simple edge trim. Use a shower-floor tile explicitly rated for that wet use.

Budget choice: Large-format tile, not fabricated natural-stone slabs. Daltile Keystones is a porcelain mosaic family to investigate for wet-floor applications.

Daltile — Keystones porcelain mosaics

Q1 · Kitchen; remnants for bars and vanity tops

Light counters

Stock cream/off-white low-movement quartz, simple eased edge. Approve the actual slab beside the cabinet sample. Match the existing waterfall silhouette only where budget permits; retained tops are the initial-phase alternate.

Budget choice: Compare local entry-tier quartz and remnants. MSI Q is a reference line; choose the warmest compatible real sample rather than a screen swatch.

MSI — Q Quartz collection

T1 · Living rooms, bedrooms, office, dining

Tailored textiles

Ivory linen-look drapery with consistent headings and discreet tracks; stain-resistant performance upholstery in ivory, oatmeal and taupe; modest tobacco accents. Rugs large enough for front furniture legs to sit on them.

Budget choice: Standard-width panels professionally hemmed and installed; reserve custom width or motors for difficult openings.

M1 · Kitchen, baths, pendants and hardware

Controlled metal mix

Satin nickel for plumbing and cabinet pulls; a limited brushed-bronze family for decorative pendants, mirror frames and accents. Keep existing window frames white and avoid changing every metal finish.

Budget choice: Stock hardware in consistent centers; use existing holes where feasible.

L1 · Kitchen task surfaces, vanities, bedside, built-ins

Layered, dimmable light

Diffuse opal or linen shades; concealed LED only under cabinets/shelves or in the existing loft tray. Specify compatible fixtures, drivers and dimmers together, accessible drivers and no visible LED dots.

Budget choice: Dimmers on existing circuits before adding ceiling architecture. Caséta is an optional control family; verify each LED load against Lutron compatibility guidance.

Kitchen fronts and island

Survey every door/drawer; specify hinge overlay, drawer clearances, end panels, fillers and toe-kicks. Match the oak island panel to the approved floor sample without requiring an exact grain match. Keep access to plumbing and appliances.

Save: Refinish or reface sound boxes; replace failed boxes locally. Retain the stainless chimney hood.

Great-room fireplace wall

Clad only the existing chase with calm porcelain. Keep both high transoms open, and place TV above the insert as requested. Conceal power and data in an accessible route; coordinate a soundbar shelf only if allowed.

Save: Tile field with carefully planned seams, stock lower consoles, no new fireplace or widened chimney chase.

Confirm before ordering: Installer checks fireplace-listed combustible clearances, heat exposure and TV manufacturer limits before fixing height or trim.

Office and media storage

Standard closed lower cabinets with scribed fillers and selected open oak-veneer shelves; keep the office window counter. Provide concealed cable paths, ventilated electronics storage and adequate desk clearance.

Save: Build one convincing wall per room; avoid continuous custom paneling around the whole room.

Confirm before ordering: Field dimensions, anchor locations, outlet access, ventilation and shelf spans approved in shop drawings.

Owner suite

Retail upholstered headboard and matching nightstands; concealed blackout rollers behind drapery. Reface the existing coffee bar and fit a remnant top without moving its sink or wine fridge.

Save: Spend on mattress, bedding and bedside lighting; use a retail headboard rather than a full upholstered wall.

Confirm before ordering: Curtain stack width must keep all four rear sashes usable and maintain access to doors and controls.

Stair finish

Preserve the quarter-turn, intermediate landing and straight continuation. Refinish or paint compatible surfaces, fit a runner template, and retain the open stairwell and existing guard locations.

Save: Refinish a serviceable rail instead of replacing the stair.

Confirm before ordering: Stair contractor confirms tread/riser consistency and guard/handrail compliance before changing any thickness or component.

Installation sequence

  1. Survey actual rooms first. Treat photo-derived dimensions as orientation only and record cabinet, glazing, floor and appliance measurements.
  2. Approve one physical palette, then quote a base scope and priced alternates. Keep retained items and demolition limits explicit in the bids.
  3. Coordinate appliances, cabinetry, countertop templates, electrical outlets, plumbing and curtain stacks before orders. Do not order counters before the final cabinet/appliance layout.
  4. Order long-lead fronts, fixtures and upholstery after measurements are signed off. Check delivery dimensions for the townhouse doors and stairs.
  5. Perform messy and concealed work, floor installation and cabinet work before finish paint touch-up and window treatments. Install furniture and styling last.
  6. At handover, check aligned doors, consistent grout, dimmer operation, night privacy, drawer/appliance clearances, task lighting and concealed-cable access.
